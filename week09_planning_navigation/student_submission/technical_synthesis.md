A route suitable for a robot sharing space with people has to be short, reliably executable and keep a comfortable distance from people, not just found quickly. In Mission 1, A* found the optimal 13.5 m route with fewer expansions than BFS (700 vs 928), while DFS returned a valid but 111 m route, so a fast search is only useful if it also gives a good route. In Mission 2, a planned route still had to be drivable: Nav2 succeeded fastest on both maps (35.4 s open, 73.4 s constrained), while DFS’s long zig-zag route timed out on both. In Mission 3, extra spacing only helped if the route still completed: A*’s social fields failed (oscillation and controller error 105), while RRT baseline already kept 1.725 m clearance with 0 s in personal space. Mission 1’s path lengths come exactly from the grid, but Mission 2 and 3’s distance, goal error and clearance are AMCL position estimates, not ground truth. They also come from single trials, using a personal-space threshold that was an experimental choice.
